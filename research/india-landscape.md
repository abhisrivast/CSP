## 0. TL;DR: the findings that should shape CSP's India content

1. **Women's reservation is now the cleanest non-partisan campaign hook in India.**
   - The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (106th Amendment) was notified into force on **16 April 2026**, but it reserves **no seats yet**.
   - A bill to bring the quota forward to 2029, the **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026**, was **defeated in the Lok Sabha on 17 April 2026**: 298 for, 230 against, short of the 352 needed.
   - Under the 2023 law, reserved seats wait for delimitation after Census 2027. Reports put the realistic date at around **2034**.
   - A plain-language public tracker answering "33% — kab?" ("33% — when?") is a gap no peer fills. [H] (A1)
2. **The marital rape exception is heading to a final hearing soon.**
   - Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) s.63 Exception 2 still exempts husbands when the wife is 18 or over.
   - A three-judge Supreme Court bench led by CJI Surya Kant has listed the batch of challenges for final hearing. SC Observer reports **18 November 2026**. [M] (A2)
3. **Latest NCRB figures (Crime in India 2024, released 6 May 2026):**
   - **4,41,534** crimes against women, down about 1.5%; rate **64.6 per lakh women**.
   - The largest category is cruelty by husband or relatives: **1,20,227** cases.
   - Rape: **29,536** cases. Dowry deaths: **5,737**.
   - Delhi has the highest rate (130.7). [M–H] (B)
4. **NFHS-6 (2023–24, fact sheets released late May 2026):**
   - Spousal violence reported by ever-married women aged 18–49 fell to **22.3%**, from 29.2% in NFHS-5.
   - Women's internet use nearly doubled, to **64.3%**.
   - More women are online, so more are exposed to online abuse. [M] (B)
5. **Women's work:**
   - PLFS 2025 female labour force participation rate (LFPR) is **40.0%** (usual status, age 15+), against **79.1%** for men.
   - The monthly CWS measure for September 2026 is **34.6%**, and urban women are at 25.9%.
   - Women aged 6+ who do unpaid domestic work spend **289 minutes a day** on it; men spend **88** (Time Use Survey 2024). [M–H] (B)
6. **WEF Global Gender Gap Index 2026 (released 16 Sep 2026):**
   - India ranks **131 of 145**, unchanged, with a score of 64.5%.
   - Economic participation is the weakest area (41.2%). Political empowerment is India's best-ranked sub-index (67th). [H] (B)
7. **Representation:**
   - **74 women MPs** in the 18th Lok Sabha (down from 78 in 2019).
   - **390 of 4,123 MLAs** (9%) are women.
   - Women were **9.6%** of Lok Sabha 2024 candidates (ADR, Mar 2026). [H] (B)
8. **2026's Gen Z energy is real, but it is male-fronted.**
   - The Cockroach Janta Party has **25 million** Instagram followers. Its protests ended with the Education Minister's resignation on 25 July 2026.
   - It has been criticised in print for having no women office-bearers, and for sexist and transphobic slogans at its protests.
   - **The gap CSP can fill: a woman-led, explicitly feminist, joyful, inclusive youth political-cultural movement with a published code of conduct.** [H for facts; the gap is my analysis] (A14, C)
9. **Protest space has shrunk.**
   - **Jantar Mantar was de-notified as a protest site on 11 October 2026.** Delhi's designated sites are now Ramlila Ground, Burari Ground and CBD Ground.
   - At least 5,000 people were detained in Delhi between 10 and 13 Oct, according to BehanBox.
   - Three women journalists alleged sexual harassment by police during the 3 Oct protest.
   - CSP's event and safety guidance must reflect this. [H] (A14)
10. **Digital safety has new rules.**
    - **IT Rules amendment in force 20 Feb 2026:**
      - AI-generated ("synthetically generated") content must be labelled.
      - Platforms must act on certain lawful orders within 3 hours.
      - AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery is expressly prohibited.
    - MeitY sent X a notice in January 2026 over Grok being used to create sexualised images of women. [H] (A13)
11. **Platform reality** (DataReportal, late-2025 ad reach):
    - YouTube **500M**, Instagram **481M**, Facebook 403M, Snapchat **213M**, X only **22.2M**.
    - Women are a minority of every platform's ad audience: Instagram about 30% female, X about 13%.
    - **TikTok has been blocked since 29 June 2020**, and MeitY said in Aug 2025 that no order to lift the block has been issued.
    - YouTube is the top news source (58%, Reuters DNR 2026, an English-speaking sample). [H] (D)
12. **Language.**
    - Hindi is the mother tongue of **43.63%** of Indians (Census 2011).
    - **98%** of internet users access Indic-language content (IAMAI-Kantar, 2024 data).
    - FII, BehanBox and Khabar Lahariya all publish in Hindi.
    - **Recommendation:** ship a Hindi `/hi/` version of the core pages soon after launch. Use Hinglish in social media and in FAQ phrasing, not as a separate locale. [H for data; the recommendation is mine] (D, E)
13. **SEO openings.**
    - English Wikipedia's *Feminism in India* article does not cover 2023–2026 events.
    - FII's "four waves" explainer is sponsored partner content with no Indian examples.
    - "Fourth wave feminism India" results are dominated by 2018 #MeToo articles.
    - Hinglish "feminism kya hai" results are weak.
    - Policy queries are dominated by UPSC exam-prep sites, which are factual but dry. [M] (E)
14. **The attitudes gap is wide, so lead with rights and behaviours, not labels.**
    - Pew (fieldwork 2019–20) found about **9 in 10** Indian adults agree a wife must always obey her husband, and **80%** say men have more right to jobs when jobs are scarce.
    - But 62% say childcare should be shared.
    - The CSDS-KAS youth survey (published 2017) found **57%** of young men and **40%** of young women held patriarchal views.
    - Ipsos 2026 found 68% of urban Indians call themselves feminists (see `audience.md`). [H] (D)

### A3. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) changes that affect women

- **Commencement:** the BNS, BNSS and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act. The BNS has **358 sections in 20 chapters** (the IPC had 511 in 23), adds **20 new offences** and raises imprisonment in 33 offences. Source: [PIB explainer PDF, May 2024](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/may/doc2024522337701.pdf). [H]
- **s.69, sexual intercourse by deceitful means:** this is new. It covers a false promise to marry, and "deceitful means" includes false promises of a job or promotion, or marrying by hiding identity. The maximum is 10 years plus a fine. Source: PIB (above). [H]
- **s.70(2), gang rape of a woman under 18:** punishable by life imprisonment, meaning the rest of the convict's natural life, or by death. Source: PIB (above). [H]
- **Marital rape exception:** kept in s.63, with the wife's age threshold set at 18 (A2). [H]
- **Dowry death** is now **BNS s.80** (formerly IPC s.304B). Source: [Kerala Kaumudi, 19 May 2026](https://www.keralakaumudi.com/en/india/general/16-married-women-die-every-day-in-india-due-to-dowry-related-violence-report-1749955). [M]
- **NCRB category change:** the NCRB introduced a new category, "Offences against Women and Child", under the BNS. Source: [Vision IAS, 7 May 2026](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-05-07/society/ncrb-releases-crime-in-india-2024-report). [M]
- **Prohibitory orders:** police use **BNSS s.163** to impose them, as at Jantar Mantar in Oct 2026 (A14). Source: [Telangana Today/PTI, 4 Oct 2026](https://telanganatoday.com/jantar-mantar-protest-3-journalists-allege-sexual-misconduct-by-police-personnel). [H]
- **Statistical caution:** 2024 is a transition year between IPC and BNS recording, and simple "hurt" became non-cognisable under the BNS. Comparisons of 2024 with 2023 need care. Source: Vision IAS search summary of NCRB 2024 coverage. [M]

### A4. The RG Kar case (Kolkata, August 2024) and "Reclaim the Night"

  - Marchers blew conch shells and used the slogan "The Night is Ours".
  - Separately, a group posing as protesters vandalised the hospital that night.
  - Source: [ETV Bharat, 15 Aug 2024](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/kolkata-doctor-rape-murder-case-reclaim-the-night-enn24081500790). [H]
- **Trial:** Sanjay Roy was convicted on 18 Jan 2025. On **20 Jan 2025** the Sealdah sessions court (Judge Anirban Das) sentenced him to **life imprisonment** and ordered the state to pay ₹17 lakh to the family. Source: [News on AIR, 20 Jan 2025](https://newsonair.gov.in/accused-sanjay-roy-sentenced-to-life-imprisonment-in-rg-kar-medical-college-rape-murder-case). [H]

On **28 Aug 2026** a bench of Justices Shampa Sarkar and Tirthankar Ghosh ordered the CBI to finish its further probe within three weeks, with the next hearing on 24 Sep. Source: News on AIR (above). [H] I found no report on the outcome after September.
- **For CSP:** "Reclaim the Night" is the closest recent Indian template for a **positive, mass, women-led, non-party** action: night-time presence, public joy and solidarity. It is also a lesson in how quickly such actions get infiltrated or politicised.

### A6. Manipur, 2023–2026

- **The violence:** ethnic violence between Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities began in May 2023. Reports put the toll at **at least 260 killed** and about **60,000 displaced**. Sources: [Eastern Mirror, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.easternmirrornagaland.com/presidents-rule-revoked-in-manipur-ahead-of-government-formation); [Vision IAS / The Hindu summary, 14 Feb 2025](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2025-02-14/the-hindu/polity-and-governance/strife-hit-manipur-placed-under-presidents-rule). ### A7. Bilkis Bano

- **The crime and release:** 11 men were convicted of gang-raping Bilkis Bano and murdering 14 members of her family during the 2002 Gujarat riots. They were released on **15 Aug 2022** after Gujarat granted remission.
- **8 Jan 2024:** the Supreme Court bench of Justices B.V. Nagarathna and Ujjal Bhuyan **quashed the remission**. Gujarat lacked the power to grant it, since Maharashtra, where the trial was held, was the appropriate government. The Court also found that the May 2022 SC order had been obtained by suppressing facts.
- **21 Jan 2024:** the convicts surrendered at Godhra sub-jail.
- Source: [Scroll, 22 Jan 2024](https://scroll.in/latest/1062500/bilkis-bano-case-11-convicts-surrender-at-godhra-sub-jail). [H]
- **For CSP:** this is a constitutional-remedy story about the rule of law working.

### A9. Acid attacks

Source: [The Tribune, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/delhi-only-ut-to-report-acid-attack-cases-ncrb-data). [M]

### A13. Digital safety and deepfakes

- **Nov 2023:** a deepfake of actor Rashmika Mandanna went viral. It swapped her face onto footage of British-Indian influencer Zara Patel. Delhi Police arrested the 24-year-old creator on **20 Jan 2024** under IPC ss.465/469 and IT Act ss.66C/66E. Source: [The Tribune, 20 Jan 2024](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/delhi-police-arrest-rashmika-deepfake-video-creator-in-ap-583115). [H]
- **Jan 2026:** MeitY sent X a formal notice after Grok was used to create **sexualised images of women without consent**. It demanded an action-taken report within 72 hours and warned that X's safe-harbour protection was at risk. Source: [Storyboard18, first published 5 Jan 2026](https://storyboard18.com/social-media/india-issues-formal-notice-to-x-over-alleged-misuse-of-grok-ai-tools-86978.htm). [H]
- **IT Rules amendment (notified 10 Feb 2026, in force 20 Feb 2026).**
  - Scope: rules for **synthetically generated information**, meaning AI-created or AI-altered content that appears real.
  - Labelling: such content must carry a **prominent label** and **embedded metadata or identifiers**, and platforms may not strip them.
  - Prohibition: AI-generated **non-consensual intimate imagery** is expressly prohibited.
  - Takedowns: some lawful orders must be acted on within **3 hours** (previously 36). Grievance responses are due within **7 days** (previously 15).
  - Source: [Business Today, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/story/govt-tightens-rules-on-ai-generated-content-mandates-labels-and-faster-takedowns-515505-2026-02-10). [H]
  - The Internet Freedom Foundation criticised the definition as overbroad (search result only). [L]
- **Reporting route:** the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) and helpline **1930**. I saw this only in search results, so check it on the official portal before publishing. [M]

### C2. What Indian Gen Z responds to (evidence)

  - In a Meta-linked Ipsos study (3,500+ people, 33 centres, Sep 2025), 97% watched short video daily, and **92% of them preferred Instagram Reels**. - **Concrete, personal stakes.**
  - Among Indian Gen Z, **unemployment** is the top concern (27%).
  - 54% say they have delayed major life decisions for financial reasons.
  - **Personal safety** is a top-five stressor (31% of stressed Gen Z).
  - Source: Deloitte 2026 ([PDF, India profile, n=506 Gen Z + 300 Millennials](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone1/in/en/docs/campaigns/2026/in-2026-genz-millennial-survey-country-report-india.pdf)) [H]. The CJP's wins came on jobs and exams, not on abstractions.
- **Belonging and community.** Blank Noise's line about belonging rather than fear, and its "Action Sheroes" identity, show the appeal [H].
- **Code-mixed language** works in titles, merch and chants: *Akeli Awaara Azaad*; "Sex Pe Charcha With Parivaar"; Kolkata's *Meyera, Raat Dakhal Karo* [H].

### C3. Gaps CSP can fill (my analysis from the evidence above)

1. **A woman-led youth political-cultural movement.**
   - The biggest youth vehicle of 2026, the CJP, is male-fronted, and feminist outlets have documented sexism at its protests.
   - CSP can show visible women's and queer leadership, and publish a **protest and online code of conduct**: no gendered slurs, no body-shaming, no "wear bangles"-style insults, and safety marshals.
   - Stay collegial rather than adversarial towards the CJP. Credit what it won, and say plainly what CSP does differently.
2. **Accountability trackers, not just commentary.**
   - FII and BehanBox report; Breakthrough and Jagori run programmes. Nobody runs Gen Z-friendly **trackers** on:
     - women's reservation ("33% — kab?");
     - the marital rape case;
     - menstrual leave by state;
     - women MPs and MLAs;
     - each year's NCRB, PLFS and NFHS release.
   - This matches CSP's "research → consciousness → action" mission.
3. **An India-grounded "fourth wave" explainer.** FII's four-waves page is sponsored content with US examples, and Wikipedia's India article stops before 2023 (E1).
4. **Hinglish explainers and Hindi pages.** Hindi exists at FII, BehanBox and Khabar Lahariya, but **Hinglish** (Roman-script Hindi, the way many young people actually type and search) is thin (E1).
5. **Legal literacy and survivor navigation.** SheSays's site is gone and Pinjra Tod is quiet online. CSP can host curated "know your rights" guides: POSH, cybercrime reporting, the new deepfake rules, protest rights after Jantar Mantar. These need **reviewing lawyers or a legal partner** and must link to existing helplines and services rather than duplicate them.
6. **Visibility outside Delhi.** Butalia's Hassan example shows how non-Delhi women's actions go unseen. CSP chapters and "case studies" can document protests in smaller cities.
7. **Men and boys as allies.** Butalia notes young men's curiosity. The CSDS-KAS survey found young men with close female friends were less patriarchal (63% vs 46%) ([The News Minute, 7 Apr 2017](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/news/young-indians-are-homophobic-misogynist-and-orthodox-says-csds-survey-60003)) [H]. That supports a friendship- and solidarity-based allyship offer.

## 7. Tone-of-voice recommendations for Indian Gen Z

The voice should be **positive, constitutional, hopeful and not partisan**. These are recommendations drawn from the evidence above, and the example lines are mine.

1. **Anchor in the Constitution, not in parties.** Use the language everyone already owns:
   - Article 14's right to equality;
   - Article 21's right to life with dignity, which the Karnataka HC applied to menstrual health;
   - "equal pay for equal work" and "maternity relief" in the Directive Principles;
   - the Fundamental Duty "to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women" ([Wikipedia: Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Rights,_Directive_Principles_and_Fundamental_Duties_of_India); [DLA Piper on the Karnataka HC](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/karnataka-high-court-mandates-strict-enforcement-of-menstrual-leave-policy)).
   - *Example:* "Barabari humare Samvidhan mein likhi hai. Ab use humari zindagi mein likhna hai." ("Equality is written in our Constitution. Now let's write it into our lives.")
2. **Judge outcomes, not tribes.**
   - Criticise results and institutional performance ("74 of 543 MPs are women"), not parties or leaders as people.
   - Credit progress wherever it comes from: Karnataka's menstrual leave policy, NFHS-6's fall in spousal violence, the Supreme Court's Bilkis Bano ruling, MoSPI publishing time-use data.
   - When a vote splits on party lines (the 131st Amendment), report each side's stated reason in one line each.
3. **Hope with evidence.** Pair every hard number with something people can do: "4.41 lakh cases were registered in 2024, and only a third of harassed women report. Here's how to report, and here's who will help."
4. **Joy is a strategy.**
   - Borrow young protesters' wit (Butalia, BehanBox). Use playful titles and Hinglish puns, and celebrate wins.
   - **Hard line:** humour never punches at bodies, caste, religion, queer or trans people, or women opponents. No "wear bangles" or "bhabhi" taunts: the failures FII documented at CJP protests.
5. **Bilingual by default.** English headline, Hinglish caption or social copy, Hindi page. Write Hindi in natural, contemporary register, not Sanskritised officialese.
6. **Survivor-centred and safe.**
   - Never name or picture survivors of sexual violence.
   - Use "survivor" or "victim" as the person prefers.
   - Add content notes.
   - Never speculate about cases under trial. State the court status and date.
   - Have counsel confirm the legal restrictions on identifying survivors (section 9).
7. **Precise about the law.** Say "acquitted, appeal planned", not "got away with it". Say "listed for final hearing on 18 Nov", not "SC to criminalise marital rape". Precision is what keeps a positive movement credible.
8. **Everyone's movement.**
   - Show caste, religion, region, disability, queer and trans, rural and urban diversity in stories, faces and leadership.
   - Feature stories from outside Delhi.
   - Invite boys and men in as friends and allies: "Uski azaadi, teri bhi azaadi" ("Her freedom is your freedom too"). Avoid framing women's rights only through men's relationships to them (sister, mother, daughter).

10. **Visibly independent.**
    - State non-affiliation on About and in the FAQ.
    - Publish funding sources.
    - Don't share stages or logos with any party.
    - Don't use party colours or slogans.
    - This matters doubly because "CSP" is already the acronym of the historic Congress Socialist Party and the founder has faced an "ex-Congress" framing (`founder.md` §5–6).
11. **Collegial with peers.** Link generously to FII, BehanBox, Jagori, Breakthrough and Khabar Lahariya, and to the CJP where relevant. Position CSP as **infrastructure** (research, trackers, training, public memory) that strengthens the field, not as a rival brand.
12. **Safety-first calls to action.** Before CSP promotes any protest, check the current designated sites, procedures and permissions (post-Jantar Mantar), and partner with legal and medical volunteer networks of the kind BehanBox describes.
