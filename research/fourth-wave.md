## 1. Executive summary (what CSP can stand on)

1. **The "fourth wave" is a recognised but loosely defined term.** Journalists and activists use it more than scholars do. Most accounts date it to about 2012 (Baumgardner put the start around 2008). They link it to technology and social media, intersectionality, and a renewed focus on sexual violence, harassment, rape culture and online misogyny. Kira Cochrane, a Guardian editor, called it "defined by technology" in 2013.
2. **The UK is central to the academic literature.** Chamberlain's *The Feminist Fourth Wave: Affective Temporality* (2017) is explicitly about the UK, and so are Cochrane's *All the Rebel Women* (2013) and Rivers' *Postfeminism(s) and the Arrival of the Fourth Wave* (2017). CSP is therefore building on a largely British and Irish intellectual base.
3. **The newer scholarship (2020–2025) reframes the fourth wave as a response to backlash.** It ties the wave to populism and the manosphere, and to new technologies such as AI, deepfakes and "nudify" tools. Examples are Peroni & Rodak (2020), Laura Bates' *The New Age of Sexism* (2025) and UN Women's *Tipping Point* report (Dec 2025).
4. **The wave metaphor itself is contested.** Critics say it implies a single, Western, generational feminism (Nicholson 2010; Hewitt 2010; Evans & Chamberlain 2015). CSP can use "Fourth Wave" as a banner while openly saying it is one strand in a longer, plural, global story. That approach fits CSP's "public memory" work.
5. **The global violence numbers are stark and recent:**
   - Nearly 1 in 3 women (840 million) have experienced partner or sexual violence (WHO, Nov 2025).
   - 137 women and girls a day are killed by partners or family members (UNODC/UN Women, Nov 2025).
6. **Equality data is moving slowly:**
   - Global gender parity is about 120 years away (WEF GGGR 2026, Sept 2026).
   - Women hold 27.5% of parliamentary seats (IPU, 1 Jan 2026).
   - 708 million women are outside the labour force because of unpaid care (ILO, Oct 2024).
7. **Use the NI femicide claim carefully.** "Northern Ireland is the most dangerous place in Europe to be a woman" is widely repeated. A PSNI statistical note (9 June 2026) says the claim came from a Eurostat error published by the BBC in 2019. It puts NI's female homicide rate (0.61 per 100,000) in the middle of the European range, just below England and Wales. CSP should not repeat the slogan. It can make a stronger, accurate case from NI's own survey data, police data and the 2026 PSNI misogyny review.
8. **The newest UK pay figures came out two days before this brief.** The ONS published 2026 gender pay gap data on 22 Oct 2026: 6.8% for full-time employees and 12.3% for all employees.
9. **The "Gen Z gender divide" is a real, documented trend, but the numbers are slippery.** For a positive movement, the strongest framing is "boys and young men are being targeted by an industry", not "young men are the enemy". It is supported by the Movember 2025 finding that 63% of young men watch masculinity influencers, and by the policy response to *Adolescence* (2025).

## 3. How the fourth wave differs from earlier waves

The wave labels are a Western shorthand; see Section 4 for the critiques. As usually described:

| | First wave | Second wave | Third wave | Fourth wave |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rough period | 19th c. – 1920s | Late 1960s – 1980s | Early 1990s – 2000s | ~2008/2012 – now |
| Signature demands | Suffrage, legal personhood, property and education rights | Women's liberation: workplace equality, reproductive rights, "the personal is political" | Diversity of identities, sexuality, reclaiming culture, challenging essentialism | Ending sexual violence, harassment and rape culture; online misogyny; bodily autonomy; intersectional justice; pay, care and representation |
| Main tools | Petitions, marches, militancy | Consciousness-raising groups, publishing, legal cases | Zines, music (riot grrrl), media critique | Hashtags, social platforms, viral testimony, data and digital archives, plus mass street mobilisation (Ni Una Menos, Women's Marches, Reclaim the Night) |
| Typical critique | Centred white, middle-class women | Accused of excluding Black, working-class and lesbian women | Accused of individualism and consumerism | Accused of slacktivism, digital exclusion, corporate co-option, call-out culture, celebrity focus and Global North bias |

Sources:

- Wave descriptions are paraphrased from Cochrane's book blurb (via search), the Britannica entry (search summary only, Medium) and the Wikipedia overview (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism).
- The "fourth wave as reaction to postfeminism" point is from Rivers 2017 (https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5346/).
- The "backlash/populism" framing is from Peroni & Rodak 2020 (https://opo.iisj.net/index.php/osls/article/view/1319).
- The third-wave origin is often credited to Rebecca Walker's 1992 essay "Becoming the Third Wave". I did not verify this in this pass (Low).

**What is genuinely new in the fourth wave:**

1. **Technology as both a tool and a battleground.** The same platforms that enabled #MeToo also host deepfakes, doxing and the manosphere.
2. **Survivor testimony at scale.** Individual stories are aggregated into public evidence (Everyday Sexism, #MeToo, the Hema Report, Pelicot).
3. **Intersectionality as a baseline expectation**, even where practice falls short.
4. **Global South leadership.** Argentina (Ni Una Menos), India (2012, 2024) and Spain sit alongside UK and US campaigns.
5. **A feeling-driven tempo.** Chamberlain's "affective temporality": mobilisation in bursts after outrage events.
6. **Tension between popular feminism and backlash.** Peroni & Rodak 2020; Bates 2025.

## 4. Critiques of the "wave" metaphor

1. **It flattens history into a single feminism that rises and falls.** Linda Nicholson, "Feminism in 'Waves': Useful Metaphor or Not?", *New Politics* XII(4), Winter 2010, argues the metaphor helped in the late 1960s but has outlived its usefulness. It implies one unified movement and hides the mixed state of progress. She proposes a **kaleidoscope** instead. https://newpol.org/issue_post/feminism-waves-useful-metaphor-or-not/ (High)
2. **It hides continuous, overlapping activism, especially by women of colour and working-class women.** Nancy A. Hewitt (ed.), *No Permanent Waves: Recasting Histories of U.S. Feminism* (Rutgers UP, 2010) gathers 17 essays arguing for new chronologies and more inclusive accounts of participants and agendas. An endorsement praises it for rejecting the "overly tidy generational model". https://www.ubcpress.ca/no-permanent-waves (High)
3. **It is Western and Global-North centred.** Evans & Chamberlain (2015, *Social Movement Studies*) and Phillips & Cree (2014), as summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism (Medium).
4. **It sets up generational conflict.** Each wave defines itself against the last. Rivers (2017) argues for an intergenerational approach instead: https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5346/ (High). Chamberlain (2017) reworks the metaphor to deal with this.
5. **Fourth-wave-specific critiques**, all from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism (Medium):
   - Digital access excludes poorer and disabled women.
   - Slacktivism.
   - Corporate co-option, e.g. Dove advertising.
   - Call-out culture dividing allies (Munro 2013).
   - Women of colour sidelined (Vickery 2018).
   - Celebrity-led campaigns weakening intersectionality (Looft 2017).
   - Focus on toppling individual "bogeymen" instead of structures (Burgess 2018).
   - Conservative critique of "identity politics" and reliance on the state (Joanna Williams, 2018).

**Implication for CSP:**

- Use "Fourth Wave" as a **positive, forward-looking banner**: "the wave we are building", not "the wave that happened to us".
- Acknowledge the lineage and its blind spots openly, for example in a "Why 'wave'? And why we question it" explainer.
- Chidgey's "assemblage memory" and Nicholson's "kaleidoscope" give CSP intellectual cover to remix history rather than rank generations.

## 5. Key themes (with CSP angles)

Each theme pairs evidence with a suggested CSP angle. The key numbers are in Section 6.

### 5.1 Digital activism
- **What:** Hashtag feminism, call-out culture, networked campaigns that need no formal organisations (Clark-Parsons, via Wikipedia). Peroni & Rodak (2020) see these as reframing vulnerability as a shared condition.
- **Risks:** Online harassment of activists, unpaid emotional labour, platform dependency.
- **CSP angle:** "From hashtag to policy". Every campaign should have an offline action and an institutional ask.

### 5.2 Intersectionality
- **What:** Crenshaw (1989) describes intersectionality as "a prism for seeing the way in which various forms of inequality often operate together and exacerbate each other" (quoted in the UN Women explainer, 12 Jun 2025).
- **Contested ground:** How intersectionality is practised is debated: who leads, and who carries the labour of explaining it.
- **CSP angle:** For a UK/NI/Ireland/India-facing movement, this covers class, race, caste, disability, migration status, rurality, sexuality and gender identity, and in NI and Ireland also community background and sectarian legacy. The NI angle is not in sources here; it is a suggestion.

### 5.3 #MeToo and its aftermath
- **Weinstein:**
  - Convicted 2020 (23 years).
  - NY conviction overturned 25 Apr 2024.
  - Retrial 11 Jun 2025 convicted on one count.
  - Separate California conviction (Dec 2022, 16 years).
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Weinstein (Medium).
- **Pelicot (France):** 51 men convicted on 19 Dec 2024. Gisèle Pelicot's refusal of anonymity became a global symbol, and the case fed debate on consent-based rape law. (Medium)
- **India:**
  - The Hema Committee report (2017 committee; report submitted Dec 2019; released 19 Aug 2024) described an informal group of 15 powerful men controlling work in the Malayalam film industry (Medium).
  - The RG Kar case (Aug 2024) triggered nationwide "Reclaim the Night" protests and diaspora demonstrations in 130 cities across 25 countries (Medium).
- **Global legal gap:** Over 60% of countries still lack consent-based rape laws (UN Women, 19 Nov 2025). (High)
- **CSP angle:** "After #MeToo: from testimony to institutions". Track courts, policing reform (for example the PSNI Langdale review), workplace codes and consent law.

### 5.4 Gender-based violence and femicide
- **Global:** 840 million women; 137 a day killed by partners or family. See Section 6.
- **NI:** survey data shows very high prevalence (UU "Every Voice Matters!" 2023). Police-recorded domestic abuse is around 30,000 incidents a year. There is a contested European comparison (PSNI note, June 2026) and a 2026 review finding misogyny inside the PSNI.
- **Ireland:** 52% of women have experienced sexual violence (CSO 2022). Women's Aid Femicide Watch lists 286 women killed violently since 1996 (as of 9 Jul 2026).
- **India:** NCRB recorded about 4.4 lakh crimes against women a year (2023: 4,48,211; 2024: 4,41,534). "Cruelty by husband or relatives" is the largest category.
- **CSP angle:** "Count every woman". Honest, data-literate femicide memorialisation that avoids contested league tables and links each death to an institutional ask.

### 5.5 Reproductive rights (post-Dobbs, NI, Ireland, India)
- **US:**
  - *Dobbs* (24 Jun 2022) ended the federal constitutional right to abortion.
  - The Center for Reproductive Rights says abortion is "at risk of being severely limited or has been already prohibited in 25 states and three territories" (page updated Sep 2026). https://reproductiverights.org/maps/abortion-laws-by-state/ (Medium-High)
  - Wikipedia's Dobbs article (2026) says 21 states ban or restrict abortion earlier than *Roe* allowed (Medium).
- **NI:**
  - Abortion is available up to 24 weeks (early services under 12 weeks), accessed only via BPAS and referred to the five HSC Trusts. It is free to NI residents.
  - Eight safe access zones operate (nidirect, undated page). https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/abortion-services (High)
  - The UK Supreme Court gave judgment on the NI Safe Access Zones Bill reference on 7 Dec 2022 (https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2022-0077.html). The outcome was not visible on the page I opened, but nidirect confirms zones now operate.
- **Ireland:**
  - Repeal vote 66.4% Yes (25 May 2018).
  - Termination is available on request up to 12 weeks with a 3-day wait.
  - 10,600 notifications in 2025 per the Department of Health annual report, as cited by Wikipedia (Medium). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_the_Republic_of_Ireland
- **India:**
  - The MTP Amendment Act 2021 (in force 24 Sep 2021) extended contraceptive-failure grounds to unmarried women.
  - It allows up to 24 weeks for special categories (rape survivors, minors and others), with medical boards deciding on anomalies beyond 24 weeks.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_India (Medium)
  - Wikipedia also states that the marital rape exception carried into Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita s.63, Exception 2 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marital_rape_in_India, Medium).
- **Global:**
  - UNFPA SWOP 2025, "The Real Fertility Crisis", reframes the debate as one about reproductive agency (Section 6).
  - UNFPA SWOP 2021 notes that 43 countries have no laws addressing marital rape: https://www.unfpa.org/sowp-2021/autonomy (Medium; 2021 data).
- **CSP angle:** "Bodily autonomy across borders". NI and Ireland are a rare good-news story of rapid legal change driven by movements. Use it as hope and proof of concept.

### 5.6 Online abuse and deepfake image abuse
- **Prevalence:** Technology-facilitated violence affects 16–58% of women across studies (UN Women, 18 Nov 2025).
- **Women in public life:** 70% of women human rights defenders, activists and journalists have faced online violence. Nearly 1 in 4 have experienced AI-assisted abuse such as deepfakes (UN Women *Tipping Point*, 9 Dec 2025).
- **Deepfakes:** Figures for the share of deepfakes that are non-consensual porn come from industry and press sources: 90–95% (MIT Technology Review, 2021), 98% (Security Hero, 2023) and 96% (Sensity, 2023). Use the range, and cite it as "estimates".
- **Law:**
  - **UK:** The Online Safety Act 2023 covers sharing intimate images, including those that "appear to show" a person. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 creates an offence of creating, or requesting, a purported intimate image without consent.
  - **Ireland:** Coco's Law (2020) carries up to 7 years; there were nearly 100 prosecutions by Sep 2024.
  - **US:** the TAKE IT DOWN Act (May 2025).
  - **South Korea:** criminalised possession or viewing of sexual deepfakes (Sep 2024).
  - Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake_pornography ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harassment,_Harmful_Communications_and_Related_Offences_Act_2020 (Medium)
- **Grok, Dec 2025 – Jan 2026:** One analysis estimated about 6,700 sexually suggestive or nudified images per hour on 5–6 Jan 2026. Regulators in the UK, EU, India and elsewhere responded (Medium).
- **CSP angle:** A practical, Gen-Z-first "Deepfake & image abuse survival guide" plus a policy campaign on platform accountability.

### 5.7 The care economy
- **ILO (Oct 2024):** 708 million women are outside the labour force because of unpaid care, versus 40 million men.
- **ILO (2018):** 16.4 billion hours of unpaid care are done every day, 76.2% of it by women.
- **UN Women (Jun 2026):** women do 2.5 times as much unpaid care as men.
- **India (TUS 2024):** women spend 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic work versus 88 for men.
- **Ireland:** the March 2024 referendum to replace the "woman in the home" clause with gender-neutral care recognition was rejected by 73.93%. This is a live case study of how framing matters.
- **CSP angle:** "Care is infrastructure". Positive, solution-led content, such as UN Women's figure that care services could create nearly 300 million jobs by 2035.

### 5.8 Pay and pension gaps
- **UK:** ONS 2026 (22 Oct 2026): 6.8% full-time and 12.3% all employees. The gap widens sharply after 40, reaching 11.0% for full-time workers aged 50–59.
- **NI:** 7.2% (all employees, 2025).
- **Ireland:** 9.6% (2022).
- **EU:** 11.1% (2024).
- **Pension gaps:** I could not verify current UK or Irish figures in this pass. The DWP and CSO pages were not reachable. See Section 9.
- **CSP angle:** "The motherhood and midlife penalty". The age profile of the ONS data is a strong Gen Z hook: "the gap is almost zero at 22 and opens up later. What happens in between is a political choice."

### 5.9 Period poverty
- **Global:** an estimated 500 million women and girls lack access to menstrual products and adequate facilities (World Bank, 12 May 2022; older estimate).
- **Scotland:** made free provision a legal duty (Royal Assent 12 Jan 2021; in force 15 Aug 2022).
- **India:** NFHS-5 (2019–21) found 77.3% of women aged 15–24 use hygienic methods, up from 57.5% in NFHS-4 (Singh & Singh, *Frontiers in Reproductive Health*, 30 Jul 2025).
- **NI and Ireland:** I could not verify the current statutory position in this pass. Check whether NI's Period Products (Free Provision) Act (NI) 2022 has been commenced.
- **CSP angle:** "Period dignity". A tangible, local, winnable campaign that suits a positive movement.

### 5.10 Political representation
- **Global:** women hold 27.5% of parliamentary seats (1 Jan 2026). Chambers with quotas average 30.9% women versus 23.3% without (IPU, via BGIPU, 6 Mar 2026).
- **UK:** 263 women MPs (~40%) in 2024, a record.
- **NI:** 32 of 90 MLAs (35.6%) in 2022, a record. The First Minister and deputy First Minister are both women, and 6 of the 12 Executive ministers are women (IfG, updated 6 Oct 2025).
- **Ireland:** 44 women TDs (25.3%) in 2024, a record. The 40% candidate quota applied for the first time.
- **India:**
  - 74 women in the 2024 Lok Sabha (13.6%); now 75 after the Wayanad by-election.
  - The reservation law is stalled pending census and delimitation.
  - WEF 2026 gives India 16.1% parliamentary parity and 5.9% ministerial parity.
- **CSP angle:** A "Representation Lab" that tracks candidates, quotas and the abuse of women politicians.

### 5.11 The manosphere and the Gen Z gender divide
- **Evidence:**
  - Movember (2025): 63% of 3,000+ young men aged 16–25 in the UK, US and Australia watch "men and masculinity influencers". 27% of those viewers report feelings of worthlessness. https://uk.movember.com/movember-institute/masculinities-report (Medium; undated page, © 2025)
  - Young women under 30 in Western countries lean left and young men lean right. The gap has widened since the mid-2010s and also appears in South Korea and China (Economist 2024, as summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_Z; Medium-Low).
  - *Adolescence* (13 Mar 2025) drew 96.7 million views in three weeks, was made free for UK secondary schools, and prompted a Women and Equalities Committee inquiry, "Misogyny: the Manosphere and online content" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manosphere ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescence_(TV_series); Medium).
- **Still to verify:** the widely shared Ipsos / King's College London (2025) finding that Gen Z men are more likely than baby boomers to agree a wife should obey her husband. I could not open the primary source; see Section 9.
- **CSP angle:** As a positive movement, CSP should run an explicit **"boys and men as allies"** pillar. Target the influencer economy, not boys.

### 5.12 AI and gender
- Women make up about 30% of the AI workforce (WEF GGGR 2023, cited by UN Women, 28 Jun 2024).
- A Berkeley Haas study of 133 AI systems found 44% showed gender bias and 25% showed both gender and racial bias (via UN Women). https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/explainer/artificial-intelligence-and-gender-equality (Medium)
- The newest risk area is AI-assisted harassment, which affects nearly 1 in 4 surveyed women HRDs, activists and journalists (UN Women, Dec 2025). Bates (2025) is the accessible book on this.
- UNESCO's 2024 study on LLM gender stereotypes is relevant, but the page blocked access, so it is unverified.
- **CSP angle:** "Feminist AI literacy". Teach Gen Z and Gen Alpha to audit the tools they use. CSP could also publish its own AI use policy as a credibility signal.

Each pillar follows CSP's own chain: research, then public consciousness, then collective action. Each is framed positively and Gen-Z-first, and can become a hub page, a blog series and a campaign.

1. **"The Fourth Wave, Explained" (Public Memory pillar).**
   - A living, plural timeline (Section 2.2) and an honest "why we question the wave" explainer.
   - Draws on Chamberlain, Chidgey and Nicholson's kaleidoscope.
   - Hook: "Every generation remixes the last. Here is our remix."
2. **"Count Every Woman" (Femicide, memory and accountability).**
   - Data-literate femicide memorialisation across NI, Ireland, the UK and India.
   - Uses PSNI, UNODC, Women's Aid and NCRB data, and corrects myths rather than spreading them.
   - Paired with institutional asks such as implementing the PSNI Langdale review and the EVAWG framework.
3. **"After #MeToo" (Testimony into institutions).**
   - Consent-based rape law (over 60% of countries lack it), court outcomes (Weinstein, Pelicot), workplace inquiries (Hema), and policing culture.
4. **"Safe Online" (Image-based abuse, deepfakes and AI).**
   - A survivor-centred guide plus a platform-accountability campaign.
   - Built on UN Women's 16–58% and 1-in-4 figures, the UK 2025 creation offence, Coco's Law and the Grok case.
5. **"Care Is Infrastructure" (Care economy).**
   - Figures: 708 million women out of work because of care; 16.4 billion unpaid hours a day; India's 289 versus 88 minutes.
   - Ireland's 2024 referendum as a lesson in framing.
   - The positive jobs case: up to 300 million care jobs by 2035.
6. **"Mind the Gaps" (Pay, pensions and power).**
   - Annual "Gap Day" content anchored on the ONS release each October (2026: 6.8% full-time, 12.3% all employees), NISRA (7.2%), CSO (9.6%) and the EU (11.1%).
   - The midlife/motherhood penalty story uses the ONS age profile.
7. **"Bodily Autonomy Across Borders" (Reproductive justice).**
   - Compare NI (24 weeks, safe access zones), Ireland (Repeal plus 12 weeks), India (MTP 2021; marital rape exception) and the US after Dobbs.
   - Positive framing: change is possible, and NI and Ireland prove it.
8. **"Boys, Men and the Manosphere" (Allies pillar).**
   - The 63% Movember finding, *Adolescence* and the Gen Z divide.
   - Positive-masculinity resources for schools and parents. Name the influencer economy, not boys, as the problem.
   - Matches CSP's "positive movement" ethos.
9. **"Representation Lab" (Women in politics and institutions).**
   - Track women's representation and abuse of women politicians in NI (35.6%), the Dáil (25.3% under a 40% quota), Westminster (~40%) and the Lok Sabha (13.6%, with reservation stalled).
   - Includes a candidate pipeline and "how to stand" toolkit.
10. **"Period Dignity" (Local, winnable campaigns).**
    - Uses Scotland's world-first Act, India's NFHS-5 progress (77.3%) and the World Bank's 500 million estimate.
    - A campaign to check that NI's 2022 Act is actually delivered (to be verified).

*Possible 11th or cross-cutting strand:* **"Feminist AI Literacy"**, teaching Gen Z and Gen Alpha to audit AI tools for bias. Uses UN Women/WEF 2023 (30% of the AI workforce), Berkeley Haas (44% of systems biased) and Bates (2025).

## 8. Glossary: 20 terms in plain language

The definitions are paraphrased from the cited source, and the source is named in each entry.

1. **Fourth-wave feminism:** The feminist activism that began around 2012. It uses digital tools and street protest to challenge sexual violence, harassment, online misogyny and inequality, with an intersectional lens. *Source:* Cochrane via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kira_Cochrane ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism
2. **Wave metaphor:** The habit of describing feminist history as successive "waves". It was popularised by a 1968 *New York Times Magazine* article and is now criticised for oversimplifying history. *Source:* https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/ads-l/2008-May/081888.html ; https://newpol.org/issue_post/feminism-waves-useful-metaphor-or-not/
3. **Postfeminism:** The belief that gender equality has already been achieved, so feminism is no longer needed. The fourth wave partly grew up in reaction to this idea. *Source:* Rivers 2017, https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5346/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism
4. **Intersectionality:** A way of seeing how sexism, racism, classism, ableism and other inequalities overlap and intensify each other, so that the experience "is not just the sum of its parts". Coined by Kimberlé Crenshaw in 1989. *Source:* https://www.unwomen.org/en/news/stories/2020/6/explainer-intersectional-feminism-what-it-means-and-why-it-matters
5. **Affective temporality:** Prudence Chamberlain's idea that shared feelings such as outrage and disbelief drive bursts of feminist action, connecting past struggles and future goals in the present. *Source:* https://abebooks.com/9783319852157/Feminist-Fourth-Wave-Affective-Temporality-3319852159/plp
6. **Assemblage memory:** Red Chidgey's term for how images, slogans and feelings from past feminist struggles are remixed and reused in new campaigns, media and products. *Source:* https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/019162264 and publisher summary.
7. **Hashtag feminism / call-out culture:** Using hashtags and public naming online to share experiences, build solidarity and hold people to account. Critics say it can also divide allies. *Source:* Peroni & Rodak 2020, https://opo.iisj.net/index.php/osls/article/view/1319 ; Munro 2013 via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth-wave_feminism
8. **Gender-based violence (GBV):** Violence aimed at a woman because she is a woman, or that affects women disproportionately. It covers physical, sexual and mental harm, threats, coercion and loss of liberty. *Source:* https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/faqs-types-of-violence-against-women-and-girls
9. **Femicide:** The intentional killing of a woman or girl because she is a woman or girl, described as the most extreme form of gender-based violence. *Source:* same UN Women FAQ (17 Nov 2025). UNODC statistics count partner/family killings.
10. **Coercive control:** A pattern of abusive behaviour, such as threats, intimidation, isolation, monitoring and controlling money, used to dominate a partner. It is a specific crime in NI under the Domestic Abuse and Civil Proceedings Act 2021. *Source:* plain-language wording based on UN Women's definitions of psychological and economic violence (same FAQ) and CJINI, https://www.cjini.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/CJI-DACPA-Yr-2-Infographics-Final-tagged.pdf
11. **Sexual harassment:** Unwanted conduct of a sexual nature, whether verbal, non-verbal, physical or online. Examples include sexual jokes, unwelcome touching and comments about someone's body. *Source:* UN Women FAQ (above).
12. **Consent-based rape law:** A law that defines rape as sex without consent, rather than requiring proof of force. Over 60% of countries still do not have one. *Source:* UN Women FAQ (rape definition) and https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/facts-and-figures/facts-and-figures-ending-violence-against-women
13. **Technology-facilitated gender-based violence (TFGBV):** Harm done, helped or amplified through phones, apps, platforms or AI, such as stalking, trolling and GPS tracking. It can spill into offline harm. *Source:* UN Women FAQ (above).
14. **Image-based sexual abuse:** Taking, sharing or threatening to share intimate images of someone without consent, including fake ones. It is a crime in Ireland under Coco's Law and in the UK under the Online Safety Act 2023 and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. *Source:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harassment,_Harmful_Communications_and_Related_Offences_Act_2020 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake_pornography
15. **Deepfake:** AI-generated or AI-altered images, video or audio that make it look as if someone said or did something they did not. Most deepfakes online are non-consensual sexual images of women. *Source:* https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/ai-powered-online-abuse-how-ai-is-amplifying-violence-against-women-and-what-can-stop-it
16. **Doxing:** Publishing someone's private or identifying information, such as their address, to intimidate or endanger them. *Source:* UN Women FAQ (above).
17. **Manosphere:** A loose online network of influencers, forums and content promoting resentment of women and rigid "alpha" masculinity. Andrew Tate is often cited as its best-known figure. *Source:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manosphere ; Movember, https://uk.movember.com/movember-institute/masculinities-report (plain-language wording by this brief)
18. **Unpaid care work:** The unpaid cooking, cleaning and care for children, older people and sick relatives that keeps societies running. Women do about three-quarters of it. *Source:* ILO 2018, https://www.ilo.org/global/about-the-ilo/newsroom/news/WCMS_633115/lang--en/index.htm
19. **Gender pay gap:** The difference between men's and women's average (median) hourly pay across all jobs. It is not the same as unequal pay for the same job, which is already illegal in the UK. *Source:* ONS, https://www.ons.gov.uk/employmentandlabourmarket/peopleinwork/earningsandworkinghours/bulletins/genderpaygapintheuk/latest (the legal point about equal pay is general knowledge and was not checked in this pass)
20. **Period poverty:** Not being able to get proper menstrual products, or the education to use them, often because of cost. *Source:* https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_poverty ; related "menstrual hygiene management" definition from the World Bank, https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/water/brief/menstrual-health-and-hygiene

**Bonus terms** (sourced and ready if needed):

- **Bodily autonomy:** Having the power and agency to make choices about your own body and future, without violence or coercion, including whether, when and with whom to have sex or become pregnant. *Source:* UNFPA, https://www.unfpa.org/sowp-2021/autonomy
- **Reproductive justice:** The human right to keep personal bodily autonomy, to have children, not to have children, and to parent in safe and sustainable communities. Coined in 1994 by Black women in the US. *Source:* SisterSong, https://www.sistersong.net/reproductive-justice
- **Safe access zone:** A legally protected area around a clinic where it is illegal to influence, record, harass or block people using or providing abortion services. *Source:* nidirect, https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/abortion-services
- **Gender quota:** A rule requiring a minimum share of women candidates or seats. Chambers with quotas average 30.9% women versus 23.3% without. Ireland's quota requires 40% women candidates. *Source:* IPU via BGIPU; Kavanagh 2025.

## 10. Copy and claims guidance for the website

- **Prefer "nearly 1 in 3 women worldwide have experienced partner or sexual violence" (WHO 2025)** over older versions. Make clear it combines partner and non-partner sexual violence.
- **Do not say "NI is the most dangerous place in Europe for women".** Say instead, for example: "In NI, almost 98% of women surveyed in 2023 reported experiencing violence or abuse in their lifetime, and police record around 30,000 domestic abuse incidents a year" (NI3 and NI1). The PSNI's own 2026 review found sexism and misogyny inside the force.
- **The pay gap is not "equal pay for equal work".** Describe it as a structural gap across all jobs, and use the ONS age profile.
- **Present deepfake prevalence as a range of estimates (90–99%)** from named sources, not as a single "fact".
- **Date-stamp every statistic on the site**, for example "(ONS, Oct 2026)". Set a reminder to refresh each October (ONS, NISRA), each March (IPU) and each June/September (WEF, UNFPA).
