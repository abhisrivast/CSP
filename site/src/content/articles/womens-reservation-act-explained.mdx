The Women's Reservation Act is a 2023 change to India's Constitution that reserves one-third of the seats in the Lok Sabha, every state assembly and the Delhi Assembly for women. It passed with almost no opposition and has been in force since 16 April 2026, but not a single seat is reserved yet, because the seats can only be chosen after Census 2027 and a fresh redrawing of constituencies. Here is what passed, what has stalled, and what you can do while the country waits.

## What does the law actually say?

Its official name is the Constitution (106th Amendment) Act, 2023. The government calls it the *Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam*, roughly "the Act honouring women's power". Most people just say *mahila arakshan*: [women's reservation](/resources/glossary/#womens-reservation).

In plain words, it does five things:

1. **One-third of seats for women** in the Lok Sabha (the directly elected house of Parliament), the state legislative assemblies and the Delhi Assembly.
2. **A quota inside the quota.** One-third of the seats already reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes go to SC and ST women.
3. **Seats rotate.** Which constituencies are reserved changes after each delimitation, so no seat is "the women's seat" for ever.
4. **It has an end date.** The reservation lasts 15 years from when it starts, and longer only if Parliament passes a law to extend it.
5. **It waits for a census.** It starts after the first census taken once the law is in force, when a delimitation based on that census picks the seats.

What it does not do matters too. It does not cover the Rajya Sabha or the state legislative councils, and it has no separate quota for women from Other Backward Classes (OBCs). Parliamentary committees that studied earlier versions of the bill had asked for both to be considered, PRS Legislative Research notes.

## How did Parliament vote?

Almost everyone said yes. That is rare in Indian politics, and worth remembering whenever the law is described as one side's idea.

## Why is no seat reserved yet?

Because of two words in the law: **census** and **delimitation**.

- **Census.** India's last full count was in 2011. Census 2027 began on 1 April 2026, and people are counted in February 2027.
- **Delimitation** means redrawing the boundaries of constituencies so that each one has a similar number of people. The law says women's seats are picked during the first delimitation after that census. It is slow work: the last Delimitation Commission was set up in 2002 and its orders were finalised in 2008.

The government brought the Act into force on 16 April 2026. The very next day, the Lok Sabha voted on a shortcut.

<Receipt figure="298–230" label="Lok Sabha vote on the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 17 April 2026. It needed two-thirds of the 528 members voting, which is 352, so it failed." source="LiveLaw" href="https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/lok-sabha-rejects-constitution-131st-bill-2026-on-delimitation-530736" date="Apr 2026" tone="pink" />

That bill would have enlarged the Lok Sabha to as many as 850 seats, allowed a redrawing on 2011 Census figures without waiting for 2027, and started the women's quota straight after. When it failed, the government withdrew the Delimitation Bill 2026 and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 that went with it (PRS lists the Delimitation Bill as infructuous).

The two sides, in one line each. Most opposition parties said they support the quota but objected to tying it to a population-based redraw, and several southern states fear losing their share of seats. The government said its plan to enlarge every state's seats in proportion protected them.

So when will it start? PRS Legislative Research thinks a delimitation based on Census 2027 is unlikely to be finished before the 2029 general election, so the quota is not expected to apply in 2029.

## Has reservation worked anywhere in India before?

Yes, in local government. The 73rd and 74th Amendments came into force in 1993 and reserved at least one-third of seats in village panchayats and town councils for women. Three decades later, women in elected local office are an everyday sight across rural India.

## Is this a party issue?

No, and we keep it that way. Almost every MP voted for the 2023 law; the parties disagree about how and when to start it. We don't take sides in that argument. Our demand is simple and the same for everyone in power: implement the one-third quota, publish the timetable, and count the women who stand, win and lead. ## What you can do

1. **Ask the question in public.** Write to your MP and MLA, politely and on the record: what is their timetable for women's reservation, and what are they doing about it? Keep the reply. That is a receipt.
2. **Ask parties for candidates now.** Women were under 10% of Lok Sabha candidates in 2024, and choosing candidates needs no census. Ask every party that wants your vote how many women it will field.
3. **Back women in local races.** Panchayats, ward committees, student unions and resident welfare associations are where leaders start. Volunteer, vote, or stand yourself. Our guide on [how to join a feminist movement, or start one at your college](/resources/how-to-join-or-start-a-feminist-group/) shows how to begin.
4. **Sign position 7.** Read and sign [the Fourth Wave Manifesto](/manifesto/), which says a third is the floor, not the ceiling.
5. **Send it to the group chat.** Share this explainer with "33% — kab?" and [join the movement](/join/) if you are 18 or over. Bring a friend. Yes, boys too.
