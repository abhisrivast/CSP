Feminism is the idea that people of every gender should have the same rights, freedom and safety, and the work of making that true. Most of the myths about it fall apart the moment you look at the numbers, so that is what we have done here: ten myths, ten receipts, each one linked to where it came from. Send the one your group chat needs.

<Myth n="01">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Feminism means hating men"</s>

Feminism is about fairness, not about putting anyone down. Wanting your sister to have the same curfew, pocket money and career choices as your brother says nothing bad about your brother.

And most men already agree with the basic idea. Feminists are not asking men to switch sides; most of them are already on it.

<Receipt figure="62%" label="of men across 30 countries say gender equality is important to them personally (68% of everyone)" source="King's College London and Ipsos" href="https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/gen-z-men-and-women-most-divided-on-gender-equality-global-study-shows" date="Mar 2025" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="02">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Men can't be feminists"</s>

Anyone who believes in equal rights, and acts on it, can be a feminist. In India the label is already popular with men.

The catch is that a label is not the same as a habit. In the same survey, 52% of Indian respondents agreed that "a wife should always obey her husband". Feminism is what you do at home, in class and in the group chat, not only what you call yourself.

<Receipt figure="65%" label="of men in Ipsos's urban, online India sample call themselves feminists (71% of women)" source="Ipsos and King's College London, IWD 2026" href="https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-03/IWD%202026%20Global%20Charts%20FINAL.pdf" date="2026" tone="pink" note="Ipsos says the India sample is more urban and online than India as a whole." />

</Myth>

<Myth n="03">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Feminism is a Western import"</s>

Indian women were doing feminism long before anyone called it that. In January 1848, Savitribai Phule, Jyotirao Phule and Sagunabai Kshirsagar opened a school for girls at Bhide Wada in Pune. In 1882, Tarabai Shinde's Marathi pamphlet *Stri Purush Tulana* turned the blame men heaped on women back on men.

Feminism started in many places at once. Ours has deep roots, and you can explore them in our [public memory timeline](/memory/).

<Receipt figure="1848" label="Savitribai Phule and colleagues open one of the first girls' schools started by Indians, at Bhide Wada, Pune" source="Wikipedia: Savitribai Phule" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule" date="Jan 1848" tone="marigold" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="04">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Equality has already been won"</s>

Huge progress has been made, and we celebrate it. But the job is not finished. The World Economic Forum's 2026 report finds the global gender gap is 69.2% closed, and at today's pace it would take about 120 years to close the rest.

India ranks 131st of 145 countries, with its weakest score on women's economic participation. That is not a reason for gloom; it is a to-do list.

<Receipt figure="120 yrs" label="to close the global gender gap at today's pace; India ranks 131st of 145" source="WEF Global Gender Gap Report 2026, via ThePrint" href="https://theprint.in/india/global-gender-gap-at-its-narrowest-in-20-years-but-parity-still-120-years-away-india-in-bottom-15/3044317/" date="Sep 2026" tone="ink" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="05">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Feminism only helps women"</s>

Feminism argues against rigid gender rules, and those rules squeeze boys too: don't cry, don't show affection, always be tough, never ask for help. Plenty of young men feel that pressure.

A world where boys can be gentle, scared, close to their friends and good at caring is a feminist world. Read our [guide for allies on boys, men and the manosphere](/resources/boys-men-and-the-manosphere/) for more.

<Receipt figure="30%" label="of Gen Z men say men should not say 'I love you' to their friends; 43% say young men should try to be physically tough" source="Ipsos and King's College London, IWD 2026" href="https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-03/IWD%202026%20Global%20Charts%20FINAL.pdf" date="2026" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="06">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Young men are all against equality"</s>

Headlines love a gender war, and there is a real gap between young men and young women on some questions. But the loudest voices online are not the majority.

When asked whether "a wife should always obey her husband", most Gen Z men said no. Talk to them as the normal majority they are.

<Receipt figure="69%" label="of Gen Z men across 29 countries do not agree that 'a wife should always obey her husband'" source="King's College London and Ipsos" href="https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/almost-a-third-of-gen-z-men-agree-a-wife-should-obey-her-husband" date="Mar 2026" tone="pink" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="07">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Most people are old-fashioned, so nothing will change"</s>

People tend to think everyone else is more traditional than they really are. That gap matters: if you think you are alone in wanting change, you stay quiet, and so does everyone else.

The fix is simple. Say it out loud. You will find you are in the majority more often than you think.

<Receipt figure="17% vs 35%" label="personally think women should do most of the childcare, versus how many think society expects it (29 countries)" source="Ipsos and King's College London, IWD 2026" href="https://www.ipsos.com/sites/default/files/ct/news/documents/2026-03/IWD%202026%20Global%20Charts%20FINAL.pdf" date="2026" tone="marigold" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="08">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Housework isn't real work"</s>

Cooking, cleaning and caring for children and elders keep every home and every economy running. It is work; it is just unpaid, and in India it falls overwhelmingly on women. Our data story [Care is work](/resources/care-is-work/) goes deeper.

Sharing it is not "helping out". It is doing your share.

<Receipt figure="289 min" label="a day spent by women on unpaid domestic work in India, against 88 minutes for men (ages 6 and over)" source="MoSPI Time Use Survey 2024, via ORF" href="https://orfonline.org/research/underlining-the-work-that-women-do-findings-from-time-use-survey-2024" date="2024" tone="ink" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="09">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Women already have enough power"</s>

Women in India have had an equal vote since 1950. They are still a small minority of the people who make the laws. Parliament passed one-third reservation for women in 2023, and it came into force in April 2026, but no seats will be reserved until constituencies are redrawn after the next census.

Our explainer on [the Women's Reservation Act](/resources/womens-reservation-act-explained/) tracks what happens next.

<Receipt figure="13.6%" label="of Lok Sabha MPs elected in 2024 were women: 74 of 543" source="Dataful, from Election Commission data" href="https://insights.dataful.in/articles/women-in-lok-sabha-at-138-slow-gains-persistent-gaps" date="2024" />

</Myth>

<Myth n="10">

## <span class="visually-hidden">Myth: </span><s>"Protests and hashtags never change anything"</s>

They do when they come with a clear demand. After the Delhi gang rape of December 2012, students filled Raisina Hill. The government set up the Justice Verma Committee on 23 December; it took public suggestions and reported on 23 January 2013. A new criminal law got presidential assent on 2 April 2013.

Not everything they asked for was won, but the speed proves the point. Read the full story in our case study on [Delhi 2012 and the Justice Verma Committee](/case-studies/delhi-2012-justice-verma/).

<Receipt figure="31 days" label="from setting up the Justice Verma Committee to its report, after the Delhi protests" source="PRS Legislative Research" href="https://prsindia.org/policy/report-summaries/justice-verma-committee-report-summary" date="Dec 2012 – Jan 2013" tone="marigold" />

</Myth>

## So what is feminism, then?

Strip away the myths and you are left with something most people already believe: that a girl born today should have the same chances, safety and freedom as a boy born today, wherever she is born and whoever her family is. The rest is detail, and the detail is where the work is. Start with [what the Fourth Wave of Feminism is](/resources/what-is-fourth-wave-feminism/), or learn the words in our [feminist glossary](/resources/glossary/).

## What you can do

1. **Send one myth to the group chat.** Pick the one you hear most and share this page with the receipt. Facts travel further than arguments.
2. **Ask, don't lecture.** Next time someone repeats a myth, ask "where did you hear that?" and show them the source. Curiosity wins more people than shouting.
3. **Bring a boy into it.** Share myths 05 and 06 with a brother, friend or classmate. Most boys are already on the side of fairness; tell them so.
4. **Learn the words.** Ten minutes with our [glossary](/resources/glossary/) and you can explain patriarchy, consent and care work to anyone.
5. **Make it official.** Read [the Fourth Wave Manifesto](/manifesto/), and if you are 18 or over, [join the movement](/join/). Under 18? Read, share and learn with us; it's all free.
