If someone has shared, faked or threatened to post an intimate image of you, or put your private details online, it is not your fault. Save the proof, don't reply or pay, and report it: since February 2026, platforms in India must take down intimate, nude or morphed images, including deepfakes, within 2 hours of your complaint. Then report it at cybercrime.gov.in or on 1930, and tell one person you trust. This toolkit walks you through every step.

This page is information, not legal advice. Every law and number on it links to an official source, so you can check it yourself.

## What counts as image-based abuse?

Different names, same idea: someone uses your image, body or details to frighten, shame or control you. Our [glossary](/resources/glossary/#tfgbv) calls the whole family technology-facilitated gender-based violence. The common forms:

- **Non-consensual intimate images.** Nude or sexual photos or videos shared without your consent, even if you once shared them with one person. The legal shorthand is [NCII](/resources/glossary/#ncii).
- **Morphed images and [deepfakes](/resources/glossary/#deepfake).** Your face edited or AI-generated onto a body or into a video. It is fake, and it is still abuse.
- **Sextortion.** "Pay me, send more, or I post this." The threat is the crime; the image may not even exist.
- **[Doxxing](/resources/glossary/#doxxing).** Posting your address, phone number, college, workplace or family details to scare you or set people on you.
- **Cyberstalking.** Someone watching, following, messaging or tracking you online after you have said no, or without your knowledge.
- **Impersonation.** A fake account in your name or with your photos.

## What does the law say?

Indian law has no single "deepfake" or "doxxing" offence, but several provisions can apply. Section numbers below follow the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), India's criminal code since 1 July 2024, and the IT Act 2000. Police and courts decide which apply to your case.

<div>

| What happened | Laws that may apply |
|---|---|
| Your intimate images are shared or published | IT Act s.66E (violation of privacy), s.67 and s.67A (obscene or sexually explicit material); BNS s.77 (voyeurism) |
| Someone threatens to share images unless you pay or obey (sextortion) | BNS s.308 (extortion); s.351 (criminal intimidation, including anonymous threats) |
| Someone follows, contacts or monitors you online after you say no | BNS s.78 (stalking, including monitoring a woman's internet use) |
| Sexual comments or unwanted sexual messages | BNS s.75 (sexual harassment); s.79 (words or gestures insulting a woman's modesty) |
| Your address or details are posted to scare you (doxxing) | Usually treated as stalking (s.78) or criminal intimidation (s.351) |
| False posts that damage your reputation | BNS s.356 (defamation) |

</div>

*Sources: UP Police BNS–IPC comparative table (2024); IT Act 2000 (India Code).*

If the person in an image is under 18, it is child sexual abuse material. Don't save or share it: report it at cybercrime.gov.in and call the Child Helpline on 1098.

## What must platforms do now?

The IT Rules were tightened on 20 February 2026. Platforms must label AI-generated ("synthetically generated") content and must not strip those labels, and the rules expressly prohibit AI-generated non-consensual intimate images. They also have much less time to act: the 2-hour rule for intimate and morphed images is the fastest, and these are the rest.

## How can friends help?

If a friend tells you, your first words matter more than anything else.

1. **Believe them, and say it is not their fault.** No "why did you send it?"
2. **Ask what they want** before you do anything. It is their decision.
3. **Help with the boring bits:** screenshots, links, report forms, the cybercrime.gov.in complaint.
4. **Report it too.** Under the 2026 rules, someone acting for the person in the image can complain to the platform.
5. **Never share, save or comment on the image**, even to defend them. Ask others in the group to report it and delete it.
6. **Go with them** to the police station or One Stop Centre, and check in again the next day. If they are struggling, Tele-MANAS on 14416 is free and confidential.

## What you can do

1. **Save this page now**, before you need it, and send it to the group chat. Most people look for this at 2 am, in a panic.
2. **Agree a rule with your friends:** if a private image of anyone lands in your group, nobody forwards it, everyone reports it.
3. **Lock down one account today** using the checklist above. Then help someone older do theirs.
4. **Talk to the boys in your life.** Our guide to [boys, men and the manosphere](/resources/boys-men-and-the-manosphere/) has scripts for the conversation, and our guide to [how to show up safely at a protest](/resources/protest-safety-india/) covers safety offline.
5. **Back the demand.** Position 6 of [our manifesto](/manifesto/) calls for an end to deepfake and image-based abuse. If you are 18 or over, [join the movement](/join/); if you want to organise around this, read [how to start a feminist group](/resources/how-to-join-or-start-a-feminist-group/).
