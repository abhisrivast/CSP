The best Fourth Wave reading list mixes the texts that named this wave with the Indian writing that was doing feminism long before anyone gave it a number. Here are 30 picks: three short ones to start, nine Indian classics, nine books on the Fourth Wave and its fights, six films and series, two podcasts, and one book by our founder. Each card gives the year, one line on why it matters, and a link to the source we checked.

You do not need to read all of it, or any of it, to be a feminist. Pick one, read it with a friend, and argue about it.

These three are short, clear and easy to find. You could finish all of them in a weekend.

## How do I read this with friends?

Reading alone is good. Reading together is how ideas become a movement. Try a reading circle:

1. **Pick one short text**, such as a chapter, an essay or a film.
2. **Meet for an hour**, in person or on a call, and keep it small.
3. **Bring one question each**, not a summary.
4. **End with one small action**: a post, a poster, a conversation at home.

## What you can do

1. **Choose your first read** from the three starters above, and tell one friend which one.
2. **Meet the women behind the old texts** in [12 Indian feminists your textbooks skipped](/resources/indian-feminists-history-forgot/).
3. **Learn the words** the books use with our [feminist glossary](/resources/glossary/), then bust a myth with [ten myths about feminism, and the receipts](/resources/feminism-myths-and-receipts/).
4. **Tell us what is missing.** This list will grow. Send your suggestion through our [contact page](/contact/).
5. **Read the manifesto, then argue with it.** [The Fourth Wave Manifesto](/manifesto/) is a draft for consultation, and if you are 18 or over you can [join the movement](/join/).
