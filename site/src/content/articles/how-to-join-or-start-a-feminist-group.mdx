You can join a feminist movement in five minutes: read something, share it, sign a manifesto, and if you are 18 or over, [join Cultural Shock Party](/join/). Starting your own group is not much harder. You need three people, one safe room and one shared question, plus a few simple rules so everyone feels welcome. This guide shows you how to find a group, start one at college, school, work or in your neighbourhood, and keep it going.

## How do I find a feminist movement to join?

Start where you are, and climb one rung at a time. Our engagement ladder has five rungs, and nobody has to start at the top.

Cultural Shock Party is new, so we are honest about what exists: you can read, share and [sign the Fourth Wave Manifesto](/manifesto/) today, and anyone 18 or over can [join the movement](/join/). Under-18s are welcome to learn with us; signing up needs a parent's or guardian's consent.

Look closer to home too: a women's cell at college, a students' union, a workers' collective, a library reading group. Women in India have long organised where they are. In 1972 self-employed women in Ahmedabad registered SEWA as their own trade union ([read the SEWA case study](/case-studies/sewa-self-employed-women/)), and in 2015 women students across Delhi formed Pinjra Tod against hostel curfews ([read the Pinjra Tod case study](/case-studies/pinjra-tod/)).

Before you join any group, check four things:

- **Who runs it and who funds it?** Good groups say so in public.
- **Is there a code of conduct,** and someone to go to if things go wrong?
- **Does it protect your data** and keep under-18s safe?
- **Is it peaceful and lawful,** and does it respect people who disagree?

## How do I run a reading circle?

A reading circle is the easiest group to start and the hardest to get wrong. The second wave of feminism called its version consciousness-raising: small groups talking about their own lives until the pattern became political.

1. **Pick something short:** under ten pages, or one video. Start with [our plain-language guide to the Fourth Wave](/resources/what-is-fourth-wave-feminism/), [intersectional feminism, explained](/resources/intersectional-feminism-explained/), or one position from [the manifesto](/manifesto/).
2. **Read it aloud together,** a paragraph each, so nobody needs to have done homework.
3. **Ask three questions:** What does it say? Where do we see this in our lives? What could we do about it?
4. **End with one action,** however small: share it, ask a teacher a question, write one letter.
5. **Rotate the chair** each time, so everyone learns to lead.

## How do we keep going without burning out?

Most groups don't fail because people stop caring. They fail because three people end up doing everything. To last:

- **Meet regularly, not constantly.** Monthly for a year beats daily for a fortnight.
- **Swap jobs,** and let people step back without guilt.
- **Celebrate every win** in public. Hope first, receipts always.
- **Make it fun.** Food, music, films, a poster night.
- **Look after each other.** Check in with people who go quiet. Tele-MANAS on 14416 is free if someone needs to talk.

## What you can do

1. **Climb one rung today.** Read one guide and send it to one friend. That is the whole first step.
2. **Find your two people.** Message two friends who you think would come. Use the invite above.
3. **Book a room for an hour.** College library, community hall, a corner of a café. Use the agenda above.
4. **Sign the manifesto.** Read and sign [the Fourth Wave Manifesto](/manifesto/), and bring it to your first reading circle.
5. **Join us.** If you are 18 or over, [join Cultural Shock Party](/join/). Under 18? Keep reading our [guides and explainers](/resources/), and ask a parent or guardian about signing up.
